\section{Physical network}

We consider a network with $N$ vertices and $M$ edges, divided into a
physical system and its environment. The corresponding vertex sets are
$V_x$ and $V_z$, with $V=V_x\sqcup V_z$. An arbitrary orientation gives
the incidence matrix
\begin{equation}
\mathbf B=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf B_x\\
\mathbf B_z
\end{bmatrix}
\in\mathbb R^{N\times M}.
\label{eq:incidence}
\end{equation}
Each column has $-1$ at its source and $+1$ at its target. The rows of
$\mathbf B_x$ and $\mathbf B_z$ belong to the system and environment,
respectively. The orientation defines signs, not a preferred direction
of physical transport \cite{vanderschaft2013graphs}.

The states $x\in\mathbb R^{|V_x|}$ and $z\in\mathbb R^{|V_z|}$ collect
quantities stored at the corresponding vertices. Assuming no independent
energy storage in the edges, their total energy and conjugate efforts are
\begin{equation}
H=H_x(x)+H_z(z),
\qquad e_x=\nabla_x H_x,
\qquad e_z=\nabla_z H_z.
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
The incidence matrix determines the edge efforts,
\begin{equation}
e=-\mathbf B^\top
\begin{bmatrix}e_x\\e_z\end{bmatrix},
\qquad f\in\mathbb R^M.
\label{eq:edge-port}
\end{equation}
Here $e\in\mathbb R^M$ is the vector of driving efforts and $f$ contains
the corresponding edge flows. A positive flow follows the chosen edge
orientation. Their constitutive relation depends on the physics of
each connection; it is not fixed by the graph.

\section{Dynamics and power}

We write the node dynamics with a feedback port on $x$,
\begin{align}
\dot x &= (\mathbf J_x-\mathbf R_x)e_x+\mathbf B_xf+\mathbf G u,
\label{eq:x}\\
\dot z &= (\mathbf J_z-\mathbf R_z)e_z+\mathbf B_zf,
\label{eq:z}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf J_x^\top=-\mathbf J_x$ and
$\mathbf J_z^\top=-\mathbf J_z$ describe reversible internal dynamics,
while $\mathbf R_x,\mathbf R_z$ are symmetric positive semidefinite and
describe internal dissipation. These matrices may depend on the state.
The matrix $\mathbf G$ selects the port directions. The system uses
its own conjugate output to determine the input,
\begin{equation}
y=\mathbf G^\top e_x,
\qquad u=\mathbf Qy.
\label{eq:port}
\end{equation}
The map $\mathbf Q$ represents instantaneous feedback, not an external
source of power. The energy balance follows directly \cite{vanderschaft2014}:
\begin{equation}
\dot H
=-e_x^\top\mathbf R_xe_x
-e_z^\top\mathbf R_ze_z
-e^\top f+y^\top\mathbf Qy.
\label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
For a memoryless passive edge connection, $e^\top f\geq0$; a resistive
example is $f=\mathbf K e$ with $\mathbf K\succeq0$. Without a separate
energy supply, passive feedback requires
$\mathbf Q+\mathbf Q^\top\preceq0$; lossless feedback has
$\mathbf Q^\top=-\mathbf Q$. Any active feedback must account for its
power source through additional physical dynamics. We have not selected
a particular edge constitutive law or feedback map
\cite{vanderschaft2013graphs}.

Adaptive networks can change their connections in response to the flows
they carry \cite{gross2008adaptive,berner2023adaptive,hu2013adaptation,stern2023learning}.
Whether this produces a self-maintaining organization is a separate
physical question \cite{montevil2015closure}. We leave both the
adaptation law and the conditions for persistence unspecified here.
