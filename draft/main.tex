\title{Persistence of constrained motion in port-Hamiltonian systems}
\maketitle

\section{System and environment}

Consider a physical system with state $x\in\mathbb R^n$ coupled
to an environment with state $z\in\mathbb R^m$. Their energies
are $H_x(x)$ and $H_z(z)$, with $H=H_x+H_z$. We write
\begin{align}
\dot x&=(\mathbf J_x-\mathbf R_x)\nabla_x H
+\mathbf B u+\mathbf E_xu_x, \label{eq:x}\\
\dot z&=(\mathbf J_z-\mathbf R_z)\nabla_z H
+\mathbf E_zu_z, \label{eq:z}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf J_x,\mathbf J_z$ are skew-symmetric and
$\mathbf R_x,\mathbf R_z$ are symmetric positive semidefinite.
The self-feedback port $(u,y)$ acts on $x$ through $\mathbf B(x)$.
The ports $(u_x,y_x)$ and $(u_z,y_z)$ represent the environmental
connection through $\mathbf E_x(x)$ and $\mathbf E_z(z)$.
Their outputs are
\begin{equation}
y=\mathbf B^\top\nabla_x H,\qquad
y_x=\mathbf E_x^\top\nabla_x H,\qquad
y_z=\mathbf E_z^\top\nabla_z H.
\label{eq:outputs}
\end{equation}
Neither port connection is assumed lossless. In particular,
a collocated self-feedback $u=\mathbf Q(x)y$ may both redistribute
and dissipate or inject power \cite{vanderschaft2014}.

The corresponding energy balance is
\begin{align}
\dot H={}&
-(\nabla_x H)^\top\mathbf R_x\nabla_x H
-(\nabla_z H)^\top\mathbf R_z\nabla_z H \nonumber\\
&+y^\top u+y_x^\top u_x+y_z^\top u_z.
\label{eq:power}
\end{align}
The balance accounts for power entering the system through
self-feedback and environmental exchange; an active feedback
law requires a corresponding physical energy source.

\section{Constrained flow}

Let $c(x)=0$ define a regular constraint manifold, with
$\mathbf A(x)=\partial_x c(x)$ of full row rank. The tangent
space is $\ker\mathbf A$. For a fixed environmental input,
the velocity without self-feedback is
\begin{equation}
v=(\mathbf J_x-\mathbf R_x)\nabla_x H+\mathbf E_xu_x.
\label{eq:drift}
\end{equation}
Preserving the constraint requires
\begin{equation}
\mathbf A\mathbf B u=-\mathbf A v.
\label{eq:tangency}
\end{equation}
If $\mathbf A\mathbf B$ has full row rank, the minimum-norm
correction and the resulting flow are
\begin{align}
u_*&=-(\mathbf A\mathbf B)^\dagger\mathbf A v,
\label{eq:correction}\\
\dot x&=\mathbf P v,\qquad
\mathbf P=\mathbf I-\mathbf B(\mathbf A\mathbf B)^\dagger\mathbf A.
\label{eq:projector}
\end{align}
Since $\mathbf A\mathbf P=0$ and $\mathbf P^2=\mathbf P$,
the matrix $\mathbf P$ projects onto the tangent space
along directions accessible through the self-feedback port.
It is generally oblique; $\mathbf B=\mathbf A^\top$
recovers the Euclidean orthogonal projector. The projection
is a kinematic construction, not an automatic port-Hamiltonian
interconnection. Its correction contributes the actual power
$y^\top u_*$ to Eq.~\eqref{eq:power}.

Full row rank is sufficient, not necessary, for a specified
drift. More generally, Eq.~\eqref{eq:tangency} is solvable
exactly when
\begin{equation}
-\mathbf A v\in\operatorname{im}(\mathbf A\mathbf B).
\label{eq:image}
\end{equation}
Thus the constraint fixes the admissible directions, while
the self-feedback port determines which transverse motions
can be corrected.

\section{Persistence of feedback capacity}

A finite feedback gain provides a physical restriction
beyond instantaneous tangency. Suppose a collocated
feedback $u=\mathbf Qy$ satisfies
$\|\mathbf Q\|_2\le\kappa$, where $\kappa>0$ is fixed.
Under the full-row-rank assumption above, a matrix
$\mathbf Q$ satisfying both the gain bound and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:tangency} exists at a given state precisely when
\begin{equation}
\|u_*\|^2\le\kappa^2\|y\|^2.
\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $u_*$ is the smallest input satisfying tangency,
and $\|\mathbf Qy\|\le\kappa\|y\|$.
Conversely, for $y\ne0$ the choice
$\mathbf Q=u_*y^\top/(y^\top y)$ achieves the correction
with the smallest possible operator norm; when $y=0$,
feasibility requires $u_*=0$. This criterion is
pointwise: an $x$-only feedback law may face additional
restrictions if $u_x$ depends on the unobserved environment.

The available feedback margin can be written without
introducing another matrix,
\begin{equation}
\phi(x,z)=\kappa^2 y^\top y-u_*^\top u_*.
\label{eq:margin}
\end{equation}
Here the environmental connection is assumed to specify
smooth inputs $u_x(x,z)$ and $u_z(x,z)$.
On the full-row-rank region, $\phi\ge0$ characterizes
states where a bounded collocated correction is available.
At $\phi=0$, every input preserving the constraint obeys
$u=u_*+w$ with $w\in\ker(\mathbf A\mathbf B)$ and
$u_*^\top w=0$. The gain bound forces $w=0$.
Consequently, all feasible feedback laws produce the
same $x$-velocity $\mathbf P v$ at that boundary.

A self-maintaining motion must also preserve the
availability of this correction. At a regular boundary
$\phi=0$, its necessary tangency condition is therefore
\begin{equation}
\partial_x\phi\,\mathbf P v
+\partial_z\phi\,\dot z\ge0,
\qquad c(x)=0,\quad\phi(x,z)=0.
\label{eq:persistence}
\end{equation}
This relates the constrained flow to the evolution of its
own control capacity. A negative left-hand side means
that the system inevitably loses feasible feedback,
even though it can preserve $c(x)=0$ at the current state.
The condition is necessary, not sufficient, for indefinite
persistence: the gain bound, rank, power balance, and
all other viability boundaries must remain satisfied.
Related recursive-feasibility conditions arise in
constrained control \cite{xiao2022}.

There is also a distinction between a correction chosen
from the full state $(x,z)$ and an autonomous self-feedback
$\mathbf Q(x)y(x)$. For the same $x$ and any two possible
environmental states $z_1,z_2$, the latter requires
\begin{equation}
\mathbf A\mathbf E_x
\left[u_x(x,z_1)-u_x(x,z_2)\right]=0.
\label{eq:environment}
\end{equation}
An unobserved change in the environmental input cannot
produce a change in transverse motion that an $x$-only
feedback is expected to cancel. If $u_x$ can vary freely,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:environment} reduces to
$\mathbf A\mathbf E_x=0$. This is a structural restriction
on the environmental port, independent of the chosen
Hamiltonian.

A two-state example makes the distinction explicit.
Let $x=(q,a)$, $H_x=(q^2+a^2)/2$,
$\mathbf J_x=0$, and
$\mathbf R_x=\operatorname{diag}(0,\gamma)$ with
$\gamma>0$. Choose the self-feedback port
$\mathbf B=(a,0)^\top$ and environmental inputs
$d>0$ and $j$, giving
\begin{equation}
\dot q=d+au,\qquad
\dot a=-\gamma a+j,\qquad y=aq.
\label{eq:example}
\end{equation}
For the constraint $q=1$ and $a>0$, the required
feedback is $u_*=-d/a$, realized by the dissipative
law $u=\mathbf Qy$ with $\mathbf Q=-d/a^2$
on the constraint. The gain bound becomes
$a^2\ge d/\kappa$. With $j=0$, the resource
$a$ decreases and reaches this limit in finite
time, even though $\dot q=0$ until that point.
At the boundary, sustaining feedback capacity
requires $j\ge\gamma a$. Thus preservation of the
regulated variable does not imply preservation of
the physical ability to regulate it.

The preservation of feedback capacity is one possible
mathematical expression of self-maintenance. Its link to
organizational closure \cite{montevil2015} requires a
physical model in which the mechanisms underlying
$\mathbf B$, the environmental connection, and the
available power are themselves sustained by the dynamics.
