\title{Constrained flow in port-Hamiltonian systems}
\maketitle

\section{System and environment}

Consider a physical system with state $x\in\mathbb R^n$ coupled
to an environment with state $z\in\mathbb R^m$. Their energies
are $H_x(x)$ and $H_z(z)$, with $H=H_x+H_z$. We write
\begin{align}
\dot x&=(\mathbf J_x-\mathbf R_x)\nabla_x H
+\mathbf B u+\mathbf E_xu_x, \label{eq:x}\\
\dot z&=(\mathbf J_z-\mathbf R_z)\nabla_z H
+\mathbf E_zu_z, \label{eq:z}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf J_x,\mathbf J_z$ are skew-symmetric and
$\mathbf R_x,\mathbf R_z$ are symmetric positive semidefinite.
The self-feedback port $(u,y)$ acts on $x$ through $\mathbf B(x)$.
The ports $(u_x,y_x)$ and $(u_z,y_z)$ represent the environmental
connection through $\mathbf E_x(x)$ and $\mathbf E_z(z)$.
Their outputs are
\begin{equation}
y=\mathbf B^\top\nabla_x H,\qquad
y_x=\mathbf E_x^\top\nabla_x H,\qquad
y_z=\mathbf E_z^\top\nabla_z H.
\label{eq:outputs}
\end{equation}
Neither port connection is assumed lossless. In particular,
a collocated self-feedback $u=\mathbf Q(x)y$ may both redistribute
and dissipate or inject power \cite{vanderschaft2014}.

The corresponding energy balance is
\begin{align}
\dot H={}&
-(\nabla_x H)^\top\mathbf R_x\nabla_x H
-(\nabla_z H)^\top\mathbf R_z\nabla_z H \nonumber\\
&+y^\top u+y_x^\top u_x+y_z^\top u_z.
\label{eq:power}
\end{align}
The balance accounts for power entering the system through
self-feedback and environmental exchange; an active feedback
law requires a corresponding physical energy source.

\section{Constrained flow}

Let $c(x)=0$ define a regular constraint manifold, with
$\mathbf A(x)=\partial_x c(x)$ of full row rank. The tangent
space is $\ker\mathbf A$. For a fixed environmental input,
the velocity without self-feedback is
\begin{equation}
v=(\mathbf J_x-\mathbf R_x)\nabla_x H+\mathbf E_xu_x.
\label{eq:drift}
\end{equation}
Preserving the constraint requires
\begin{equation}
\mathbf A\mathbf B u=-\mathbf A v.
\label{eq:tangency}
\end{equation}
If $\mathbf A\mathbf B$ has full row rank, the minimum-norm
correction and the resulting flow are
\begin{align}
u_*&=-(\mathbf A\mathbf B)^\dagger\mathbf A v,
\label{eq:correction}\\
\dot x&=\mathbf P v,\qquad
\mathbf P=\mathbf I-\mathbf B(\mathbf A\mathbf B)^\dagger\mathbf A.
\label{eq:projector}
\end{align}
Since $\mathbf A\mathbf P=0$ and $\mathbf P^2=\mathbf P$,
the matrix $\mathbf P$ projects onto the tangent space
along directions accessible through the self-feedback port.
It is generally oblique; $\mathbf B=\mathbf A^\top$
recovers the Euclidean orthogonal projector. This choice
minimizes the Euclidean norm of the input, not a physical
cost specified by the model. Other corrections can preserve
the same constraint and generate different tangential motions.
The projection is a kinematic construction, not an automatic
port-Hamiltonian interconnection. Its correction contributes
the actual power $y^\top u_*$ to Eq.~\eqref{eq:power}.

Full row rank is sufficient, not necessary, for a specified
drift. More generally, Eq.~\eqref{eq:tangency} is solvable
exactly when
\begin{equation}
-\mathbf A v\in\operatorname{im}(\mathbf A\mathbf B).
\label{eq:image}
\end{equation}
Thus the constraint fixes the admissible directions, while
the self-feedback port determines which transverse motions
can be corrected.

\section{Energetic compatibility of constrained flow}

A tangent projection need not preserve the physical energy
balance. Consider the internal system with environmental
inputs fixed at zero and write
$\mathbf L=\mathbf J_x-\mathbf R_x$. We seek a projector
onto $\ker\mathbf A$ for which the constrained flow is
passive for every energy gradient $h$,
\begin{equation}
h^\top\mathbf P\mathbf Lh\leq0
\qquad\text{for all }h.
\label{eq:passivity}
\end{equation}
Assume that $\mathbf L$ is invertible. This requirement
selects one projection,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf P_*=
\mathbf I-\mathbf L\mathbf A^\top
(\mathbf A\mathbf L\mathbf A^\top)^{-1}\mathbf A,
\label{eq:physicalprojector}
\end{equation}
which exists precisely when $\mathbf A\mathbf L\mathbf A^\top$
is invertible. To see uniqueness, a matrix with negative
semidefinite symmetric part has the same left and right
kernels. Applied to $\mathbf P\mathbf L$, this implies
$\ker\mathbf P=\operatorname{im}(\mathbf L\mathbf A^\top)$,
since $\operatorname{im}\mathbf P=\ker\mathbf A$.
These two complementary subspaces determine
Eq.~\eqref{eq:physicalprojector}.

The selected flow is also sufficient for
Eq.~\eqref{eq:passivity}. Writing
$h_c=h-\mathbf A^\top
(\mathbf A\mathbf L\mathbf A^\top)^{-1}
\mathbf A\mathbf Lh$, we have
$\mathbf A\mathbf Lh_c=0$, and hence
\begin{equation}
h^\top\mathbf P_*\mathbf Lh
=h_c^\top\mathbf Lh_c
=-h_c^\top\mathbf R_xh_c\leq0.
\label{eq:reducedpower}
\end{equation}
The projection respects both tangent geometry and
dissipation. For $\mathbf R_x=0$ it has the algebraic
form of Dirac reduction and is a Poisson operator
when the original $\mathbf J_x$ is Poisson.
For $\mathbf J_x=0$ it gives a mobility-weighted
projected gradient flow. These reductions are
related to established constrained Hamiltonian
theory \cite{vanderschaft2020}.

For the original self-feedback port to realize this
projection for arbitrary energy gradients, its actuation
space must contain the required correction directions,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{im}(\mathbf L\mathbf A^\top)
\subseteq\operatorname{im}\mathbf B.
\label{eq:portmatching}
\end{equation}
This condition restricts the matrices of the physical
system. It does not, by itself, supply the power needed
by the feedback mechanism.

In fact, passivity of the projected vector field is
weaker than passivity of the self-feedback port.
Suppose $y^\top u\leq0$ and $u_x=0$. If the
constraint must be invariant for every energy gradient,
take $h=\mathbf A^\top w$. Tangency implies
$h^\top\dot x=0$, whereas the energy balance gives
$y^\top u=h^\top\mathbf R_xh$. Thus passive feedback
requires
\begin{equation}
\mathbf R_x\mathbf A^\top=0.
\label{eq:obstacle}
\end{equation}
Dissipation along a constraint normal cannot be
canceled by a passive self-port without another power
source. This is related to the dissipation obstacle
in control by interconnection \cite{zhang2015}.

The figure compares two actuation geometries in
the same physical system. With $\mathbf B=\mathbf A^\top$
the constrained dynamics are
$\dot p=-1-0.4p$. With the structure-aligned
$\mathbf B=\mathbf L\mathbf A^\top$, they are
$\dot p=-2.9p$. The latter flow is passive,
but even it requires positive self-port power
at $(q,p)=(1,0)$, where $y^\top u=0.4$.
Its implementation therefore needs an energy source
outside an isolated passive self-feedback loop.

The invertibility of $\mathbf A\mathbf L\mathbf A^\top$
is an intrinsic algebraic condition, not an
independently chosen capacity measure. In the
nondegenerate lossless limit $\mathbf R_x=0$, this
matrix is skew-symmetric, so an odd number of
independent constraints cannot satisfy the condition.
A physical realization must also maintain the
port-matching condition and provide the requisite
power along the resulting motion. These are
structural prerequisites for a self-maintaining
constraint, not yet a dynamical theory of its origin.
