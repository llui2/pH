\section{Port-Hamiltonian system}

Consider two states $x \in \mathbb R^n$ and $z \in \mathbb R^m$ with
stored energies $H(x)$ and $H(z)$. We write
\begin{align}
    \dot x
    &=
    [J(x)-R(x)] \nabla H(x)
    + C(x,z) \nabla H(z)
    + G(x)u,
    \label{eq:x}\\
    \dot z
    &=
    [J(z)-R(z)] \nabla H(z)
    - C^\top(x,z) \nabla H(x),
    \label{eq:z}
\end{align}
with
\begin{equation}
    J^\top = -J,
    \qquad
    R^\top = R \succeq 0.
\end{equation}
The matrices $J$ generate conservative motion, $R$ dissipates energy,
and $C$ transfers energy between $x$ and $z$. The input $u$ acts on $x$
through $G(x)$, with conjugate output
\begin{equation}
    y
    =
    G^\top(x)\nabla H(x).
    \label{eq:y}
\end{equation}

The opposite signs of the coupling terms make the $x$--$z$ interaction
power preserving. For
\begin{equation}
    H_{\mathrm{tot}}
    =
    H(x)+H(z),
\end{equation}
the two coupling contributions cancel and
\begin{equation}
    \dot H_{\mathrm{tot}}
    =
    -\nabla H(x)^\top R(x)\nabla H(x)
    -\nabla H(z)^\top R(z)\nabla H(z)
    + y^\top u.
    \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
Thus $y^\top u$ is the power entering the $x$ system through the
remaining port.

For the constraint calculation we only abbreviate the part of the
$x$ dynamics that is present without this port,
\begin{equation}
    F(x,z)
    =
    [J(x)-R(x)]\nabla H(x)
    + C(x,z)\nabla H(z),
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
    \dot x
    =
    F(x,z)+G(x)u.
    \label{eq:compact}
\end{equation}

\section{Constraint and feedback}

Let the desired configurations satisfy
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal M
    =
    \{x:g(x)=0\},
    \qquad
    g:\mathbb R^n \to \mathbb R^r.
\end{equation}
If $x$ starts on $\mathcal M$, invariance requires
\begin{equation}
    \dot g
    =
    Dg(x)\dot x
    =
    Dg(x)\bigl[F(x,z)+G(x)u\bigr]
    =
    0.
    \label{eq:constraint}
\end{equation}
Hence the input must satisfy
\begin{equation}
    Dg(x)G(x)u
    =
    -Dg(x)F(x,z).
    \label{eq:control}
\end{equation}

If $Dg\,G$ has full row rank, the port can correct every direction that
violates the constraint. The general solution is
\begin{equation}
    u
    =
    -(Dg\,G)^\dagger Dg\,F
    +
    \left[I-(Dg\,G)^\dagger Dg\,G\right]v.
    \label{eq:u}
\end{equation}
The first term is the minimum-norm correction needed to remain on
$\mathcal M$. The second lies in the nullspace of $Dg\,G$ and therefore
does not change $\dot g$.

The same construction can make $\mathcal M$ attractive rather than only
invariant. Imposing
\begin{equation}
    \dot g
    =
    -K_g g,
    \qquad
    K_g \succ 0,
\end{equation}
gives
\begin{equation}
    u
    =
    -(Dg\,G)^\dagger
    \left(Dg\,F+K_g g\right)
    +
    \left[I-(Dg\,G)^\dagger Dg\,G\right]v.
    \label{eq:restore}
\end{equation}
On $\mathcal M$ this reduces to Eq.~\eqref{eq:u}; away from it the same
port also drives $g$ toward zero.

We now require the control to come from the output of the same port,
\begin{equation}
    u
    =
    Q(x,z)y,
    \qquad
    y
    =
    G^\top(x)\nabla H(x).
    \label{eq:self}
\end{equation}
The constraint condition becomes
\begin{equation}
    Dg\,F
    +
    Dg\,GQy
    =
    0.
    \label{eq:Qconstraint}
\end{equation}
Thus $Q$ only has to map the available output $y$ to one of the control
vectors allowed by Eq.~\eqref{eq:u}. For $y\neq0$, one simple choice for
any such $u$ is
\begin{equation}
    Q
    =
    \frac{u y^\top}{y^\top y},
    \label{eq:Q}
\end{equation}
since $Qy=u$. If $y=0$, this algebraic self-feedback cannot generate a
nonzero correction.

The energetic role of $Q$ follows directly from Eq.~\eqref{eq:energy},
\begin{equation}
    y^\top u
    =
    y^\top Qy.
    \label{eq:Qpower}
\end{equation}
Only the symmetric part of $Q$ contributes to this power. If $Q$ is
skew-symmetric, the feedback only redistributes energy and
$y^\top Qy=0$. A negative symmetric part removes energy, while a positive
symmetric part injects it.

The constraint and the energy balance are therefore separate
requirements. Equation~\eqref{eq:Qconstraint} determines the feedback
needed to maintain $g$, while Eq.~\eqref{eq:Qpower} determines the power
required to realize it. At this level $Q$ is an effective feedback law,
not an energy reservoir. If maintaining the constraint requires net
injection or removal of energy, the corresponding source or sink must be
represented explicitly by the $z$ system or by an external port.
