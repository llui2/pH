\title{Self-maintenance in open chemical networks}
\maketitle

\section{Chemical network}

Consider $N$ species and $M$ reactions, with system amounts
$x\in\mathbb R^n$ and environmental amounts $z\in\mathbb R^{N-n}$.
Reactions connect chemical complexes containing one or more species.
If there are $K$ complexes, let $\mathbf B\in\mathbb R^{K\times M}$
be their graph incidence matrix and $\mathbf Z\in\mathbb R^{N\times K}$
their species-composition matrix. The stoichiometric matrix is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf S=\mathbf Z\mathbf B=
\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf S_x\\\mathbf S_z\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:stoichiometry}
\end{equation}
Each column gives the net species change of a reaction. The complex
graph retains an incidence operator without restricting reactions to
pairwise transport \cite{vanderschaft2013balanced}.

Writing $v\in\mathbb R^M$ for the signed reaction rates, the dynamics
are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot x&=\mathbf S_xv(x,z),\\
\dot z&=\mathbf S_zv(x,z)+r.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:dynamics}
\end{equation}
The flow $r$ replenishes environmental resources and removes waste;
no external repair acts directly on $x$. Setting $r=0$ gives a closed
network.

\section{Thermodynamic structure}

At fixed temperature and pressure, let $H(x,z)$ denote the Gibbs free
energy of the combined network and define the chemical potentials
\begin{equation}
\mu=\nabla H=
\begin{bmatrix}\mu_x\\\mu_z\end{bmatrix},
\qquad A=-\mathbf S^\top\mu.
\label{eq:affinity}
\end{equation}
The affinities and reaction rates are power-conjugate. For passive
elementary reactions, the affinity $A_a$ and net rate
$v_a$ satisfy $A_av_a\geq0$, giving
\begin{equation}
\dot H=-A^\top v+\mu_z^\top r.
\label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
The first term is reaction dissipation and the second is chemical
power supplied to the environment. A finite closed network with $H$
bounded below cannot sustain positive mean dissipation indefinitely;
ongoing turnover requires environmental replenishment
\cite{vanderschaft2013thermo,rao2016crn}.

\section{Self-maintaining organization}

Catalysts constrain reaction rates without net consumption. They
cancel from the stoichiometric columns when present on both sides of a
reaction, but remain in its kinetic law. Stoichiometry alone therefore
does not describe which components enable a reaction.

Consider two system components $C_1,C_2$, environmental precursors
$F_1,F_2$, and waste $W_1,W_2$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
F_1+C_2&\rightleftharpoons C_1+C_2,
& C_1&\rightleftharpoons W_1,\\
F_2+C_1&\rightleftharpoons C_2+C_1,
& C_2&\rightleftharpoons W_2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:example}
\end{equation}
The production rates $v_1,v_2$ correspond to the first column, top to
bottom, while $v_3,v_4$ denote degradation in the second column. Each
component catalyzes the other's production.

The internal amounts $c_1,c_2$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
\dot c_1=v_1-v_3,
\qquad \dot c_2=v_2-v_4.
\label{eq:maintenance}
\end{equation}
Stationary maintenance with ongoing turnover requires $v_1=v_3>0$
and $v_2=v_4>0$. These balances do not establish persistence: kinetics,
thermodynamics, and dynamical stability must also permit the state.
No target concentration or external repair is imposed.

The question is whether a network's reactions can maintain the
components that enable those reactions. It connects chemical
organization theory and closure of constraints
\cite{dittrich2007organization,montevil2015closure}.
