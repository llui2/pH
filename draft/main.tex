\section{Physical network}

Consider a graph $G=(V,E)$ with $N$ vertices and $M$ edges. We divide
its vertices into a system and its environment,
\begin{equation}
V = V_x \sqcup V_z,
\qquad |V_x| = n, \quad |V_z| = N-n.
\label{eq:partition}
\end{equation}
Each vertex stores one physical quantity, such as charge or an amount of
matter. The vectors $x\in\mathbb R^n$ and $z\in\mathbb R^{N-n}$ collect
these quantities in the system and environment. Edges describe permitted
transport between vertices, including connections crossing the
system--environment boundary.

Give each edge an arbitrary orientation. Its incidence matrix is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf B =
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf B_x\\
\mathbf B_z
\end{bmatrix}
\in\mathbb R^{N\times M},
\label{eq:incidence}
\end{equation}
where each column has $-1$ at the source and $+1$ at the target.
The rows of $\mathbf B_x$ and $\mathbf B_z$ correspond to $V_x$ and
$V_z$, respectively. The orientation only sets the signs of edge
quantities; it does not impose a preferred direction of transport.

Let $H_x(x)$ and $H_z(z)$ be the energies stored in the two subsystems.
For a connection with no independent energy storage, the total energy is
\begin{equation}
H(x,z) = H_x(x) + H_z(z).
\label{eq:energy-function}
\end{equation}
The corresponding efforts are $e_x=\nabla_x H_x$ and
$e_z=\nabla_z H_z$. For an edge oriented from vertex $i$ to vertex $j$,
its driving effort is $e_i-e_j$. Collecting all edge differences gives
\begin{equation}
\eta
= -\mathbf B^\top
\begin{bmatrix}e_x\\e_z\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
j = \mathbf K\eta,
\label{eq:edge-law}
\end{equation}
where $j\in\mathbb R^M$ collects the signed fluxes and
\begin{equation}
\mathbf K = \operatorname{diag}(k_1,\ldots,k_M),
\qquad k_e\geq0,
\label{eq:conductance}
\end{equation}
contains the edge conductances. Positive $j_e$ means transport in the
chosen orientation. Equation~\eqref{eq:edge-law} is the minimal
resistive constitutive law; it can describe electrical conduction or
diffusive transport with appropriate choices of state and energy.

\section{Dynamics and power}

The incidence matrix converts edge fluxes into changes of the stored
quantities. We allow each subsystem to have additional port-Hamiltonian
dynamics that are not explicitly represented by transport edges:
\begin{align}
\dot x
&= [\mathbf J_x-\mathbf R_x]e_x+\mathbf B_x j+\mathbf G u,
\label{eq:x}\\
\dot z
&= [\mathbf J_z-\mathbf R_z]e_z+\mathbf B_z j.
\label{eq:z}
\end{align}
The matrices $\mathbf J_x$ and $\mathbf J_z$ are skew-symmetric, while
$\mathbf R_x$ and $\mathbf R_z$ are symmetric positive semidefinite.
They describe reversible internal dynamics and internal dissipation,
respectively. Their dependence on the state is left implicit. The
matrix $\mathbf G(x)\in\mathbb R^{n\times p}$ specifies a separate
open port acting on $x$, with input $u\in\mathbb R^p$ and conjugate
output $y\in\mathbb R^p$,
\begin{equation}
y=\mathbf G^\top e_x.
\label{eq:port}
\end{equation}
The product $y^\top u$ is the power exchanged through that port. We
leave the relation between $u$ and $y$ unspecified: no regulator or
viability condition is assumed at this stage \cite{vanderschaft2014}.

Since $\mathbf 1^\top\mathbf B=0$, transport alone conserves the total
stored quantity across the combined graph. With
$\mathbf L=\mathbf B\mathbf K\mathbf B^\top$, the edge terms in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:x}--\eqref{eq:z} can equivalently be written as
$-\mathbf L[e_x^\top,e_z^\top]^\top$; $\mathbf L$ is the weighted
graph Laplacian. The full energy balance is
\begin{equation}
\dot H
= -e_x^\top\mathbf R_x e_x
  -e_z^\top\mathbf R_z e_z
  -\eta^\top\mathbf K\eta
  +y^\top u.
\label{eq:energy-balance}
\end{equation}
Thus the graph fixes where transport is possible, the conductances
specify its dissipative response, and the open port accounts for power
not supplied by the resolved environment. This formulation describes
resistive edge transport; reversible interconnections require additional
physical structure, for example energy-storing edge degrees of freedom.

The conductances $k_e$ may eventually change with the physical state,
but Eq.~\eqref{eq:conductance} alone does not define adaptation. If
changing a connection consumes or stores energy, that process must be
included in the physical dynamics rather than treated as a free
modulation of $\mathbf K$.
