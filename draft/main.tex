\section{A physical system and its regulator}

Consider a physical system with state $x\in\mathbb R^n$ coupled to a
regulator or environment with state $z\in\mathbb R^m$. They store
energies $H_x(x)$ and $H_z(z)$. We assume that their connections have
no independent energy storage, so that $H=H_x+H_z$. Write
$e_x=\nabla H_x$ and $e_z=\nabla H_z$ for the generalized efforts.
A port-Hamiltonian description is
\begin{align}
\dot x &= (J_x-R_x)e_x+C e_z+G u, \label{eq:x}\\
\dot z &= (J_z-R_z)e_z-C^\top e_x+B v, \label{eq:z}
\end{align}
where the matrices can depend on the state, and
\begin{equation}
J_x^\top=-J_x,\qquad J_z^\top=-J_z,\qquad
R_x=R_x^\top\succeq0,\qquad R_z=R_z^\top\succeq0.
\end{equation}
The skew terms describe reversible motion, the positive semidefinite
terms describe dissipation, and $C(x,z)$ transfers energy between the
two subsystems without storing it. The port matrices $G(x)$ and
$B(z)$ specify where the regulator can act and where its energetic
reaction enters. Their conjugate outputs are
\begin{equation}
y=G^\top e_x,\qquad h=B^\top e_z.
\label{eq:outputs}
\end{equation}
The pairs $(u,y)$ and $(v,h)$ carry powers $y^\top u$ and $h^\top v$,
respectively \cite{vanderschaft2014}. The energy balance is
\begin{equation}
\dot H=-e_x^\top R_xe_x-e_z^\top R_ze_z+y^\top u+h^\top v.
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
The terms involving $C$ cancel exactly. Equation~\eqref{eq:energy}
accounts for both sides of the regulatory connection: $u$ cannot
supply energy to $x$ without a corresponding energetic cost in $z$
or an explicitly modeled external source.

\section{The constraint and available actuation}

Let $g:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^r$ describe regulated observables
relative to their target values. The operating surface is
\begin{equation}
M=\{x:g(x)=0\}.
\end{equation}
We assume that $Dg(x)$ has full row rank near $M$, so the surface
is locally smooth. Invariance of $M$ requires $\dot g=0$ for
every initial condition on it. Define the drift of $x$ without the
regulatory input and the map from its port to the observables,
\begin{equation}
f(x,z)=(J_x-R_x)e_x+C e_z,\qquad
A(x)=Dg(x)G(x).
\label{eq:drift}
\end{equation}
Substitution into Eq.~\eqref{eq:x} gives the exact tangency condition
\begin{equation}
A(x)u=-Dg(x)f(x,z),\qquad x\in M.
\label{eq:tangency}
\end{equation}
A regulating input exists at a given $(x,z)$ precisely when
$-Dg f$ belongs to the image of $A$. Full row rank of $A$ guarantees
solvability for any transverse drift. Under this assumption the
inputs have the form
\begin{equation}
u=-A^\dagger Dg f+(I-A^\dagger A)w,
\label{eq:controls}
\end{equation}
where $A^\dagger$ is the Moore--Penrose inverse. The first term is
the minimum Euclidean-norm correction; the second changes the motion
without changing $\dot g$. This is instantaneous invariance, not
attraction to $M$ or a stability result for the coupled system.

\section{Feedback and power}

To describe an algebraic feedback through the measured port, write
\begin{equation}
u=Q(x,z)y,\qquad y=G^\top e_x.
\label{eq:feedback}
\end{equation}
Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:tangency} and \eqref{eq:feedback},
the feedback must satisfy
\begin{equation}
AQy=-Dg f.
\label{eq:qconstraint}
\end{equation}
For $y\ne0$, any feasible input $u$ can be represented by
$Q=u y^\top/(y^\top y)$. This representation does not establish
physical realizability: its power is
\begin{equation}
y^\top u=y^\top Q_s y,\qquad
Q_s=\tfrac12(Q+Q^\top).
\label{eq:feedbackpower}
\end{equation}
The skew part of $Q$ transfers power between port components but
cannot inject energy. An input requiring $y^\top u>0$ cannot be
realized by skew feedback alone. If $y=0$, the law $u=Qy$ cannot
produce any nonzero correction, even when Eq.~\eqref{eq:tangency}
admits one.

The two requirements can conflict even when the actuation matrix
has full row rank. Let $u_c=-A^\dagger Dg f$. A power-neutral
correction requires both $Au=-Dg f$ and $y^\top u=0$. When
$y^\top(I-A^\dagger A)\ne0$, the free term in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:controls} can make the correction power-neutral.
Otherwise its power is fixed at $y^\top u_c$, so a lossless
correction exists only if this quantity vanishes. For $y\ne0$
and $y^\top u=0$, the skew map
\begin{equation}
Q=\frac{u y^\top-y u^\top}{y^\top y}
\label{eq:skew}
\end{equation}
indeed satisfies $Qy=u$. These are algebraic feasibility
statements; they do not imply that $Q$ can be implemented by
an autonomous regulator.

For a physical connection between the two systems with no external
power supply, the regulatory ports must obey
\begin{equation}
y^\top u+h^\top v\leq0.
\label{eq:closure}
\end{equation}
Equality represents lossless exchange and a strict inequality
allows dissipation in the connection. A positive power demand
$y^\top u$ must therefore be paid for through the $z$ port.
In particular, if $h=0$, an input with $y^\top u>0$ is
impossible under this passive closure. The inequality is necessary
for energetic consistency, not sufficient to construct an
interconnection or stabilize $M$.

If $H$ is bounded below by $H_{\min}$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:closure}
also gives the finite-energy restriction
\begin{equation}
\int_0^T\!
\left(e_x^\top R_xe_x+e_z^\top R_ze_z\right)dt
\leq H(0)-H_{\min}.
\label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
Thus an isolated, finite-energy regulator cannot support strictly
positive dissipation indefinitely. Sustained homeostasis against
persistent losses requires an energy supply represented in the
model, rather than hidden in the feedback matrix.

\section{Interpretation}

The operating surface $M$ is a local representation of the
essential variables in Ashby's account of regulation
\cite{ashby1956}. The good regulator theorem links effective
regulation to an internal model under its own assumptions
\cite{conant1970}. Here $z$ is a candidate physical carrier of
regulatory dynamics, but the existence of the state $z$ or the
feedback matrix $Q$ does not establish that it contains an
internal model in the sense of the theorem or of the internal
model principle \cite{francis1976}.

The immediate problem is to determine whether a closed $x$--$z$
dynamics can satisfy both the tangency condition
\eqref{eq:tangency} and the power condition \eqref{eq:closure},
and whether the resulting trajectories remain near $M$ under
perturbations. The present equations identify the geometric and
energetic requirements, but do not yet provide a dynamical
regulation theorem or a new physical mechanism.
