\section{Physical network}

Consider a graph with $N$ vertices and $M$ edges, partitioned into a
system $V_x$ and an environment $V_z$, with $V=V_x\sqcup V_z$.
An arbitrary edge orientation defines the incidence matrix
\begin{equation}
\mathbf B=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf B_x\\
\mathbf B_z
\end{bmatrix}
\in\mathbb R^{N\times M}.
\label{eq:incidence}
\end{equation}
Each column has $-1$ at its source and $+1$ at its target; the rows
are partitioned accordingly. Edge orientation fixes signs, not a
preferred transport direction \cite{vanderschaft2013graphs}.

Node states $x\in\mathbb R^{|V_x|}$ and $z\in\mathbb R^{|V_z|}$
store one quantity per vertex. With no independent edge storage, the
energy and node efforts are
\begin{equation}
H=H_x(x)+H_z(z),
\qquad e_x=\nabla_x H_x,
\qquad e_z=\nabla_z H_z.
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
The incidence matrix determines the edge efforts,
\begin{equation}
e=-\mathbf B^\top
\begin{bmatrix}e_x\\e_z\end{bmatrix},
\qquad f\in\mathbb R^M.
\label{eq:edge-port}
\end{equation}
Here $e$ and $f$ are conjugate edge efforts and flows. The sign of
$f$ follows the chosen orientation. For each edge $a$, allow a local law
\begin{equation}
f_a=F_a(e_a;x,z),
\label{eq:edge-law}
\end{equation}
where $F_a$ depends only on the endpoint states. Passivity requires
$e_af_a\geq0$; the resistive law $f_a=k_ae_a$, $k_a\geq0$, is one
example. The graph does not prescribe the transport law.

\section{Dynamics and power}

The node dynamics are
\begin{align}
\dot x &= (\mathbf J_x-\mathbf R_x)e_x+\mathbf B_xf,
\label{eq:x}\\
\dot z &= (\mathbf J_z-\mathbf R_z)e_z+\mathbf B_zf,
\label{eq:z}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf J_x,\mathbf J_z$ are skew-symmetric and
$\mathbf R_x,\mathbf R_z$ are symmetric positive semidefinite.
These matrices describe local reversible and dissipative processes;
any off-diagonal coupling respects the corresponding graph.
The energy balance is \cite{vanderschaft2014}
\begin{equation}
\dot H
=-e_x^\top\mathbf R_xe_x
-e_z^\top\mathbf R_ze_z
-e^\top f.
\label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
The system obtains energy through its connections with the environment.
Let $\mathbf B_x^\partial$ contain the columns of $\mathbf B_x$
corresponding to edges crossing the partition, with flows $f^\partial$.
The power entering the system through these edges is
\begin{equation}
P_{z\to x}=e_x^\top\mathbf B_x^\partial f^\partial.
\label{eq:environment-power}
\end{equation}
A positive value means that $x$ gains energy from $z$. On a passive
boundary edge, the energy lost by $z$ is the energy received by $x$
plus the dissipation at that connection.

With passive edges and internal dissipation, the total energy is
nonincreasing. A finite environment whose energy is bounded below
cannot support sustained positive dissipation indefinitely; persistent
throughput requires the environment to be replenished by a physical
process. Any such supply must be included in the enlarged physical
network, rather than introduced as an independent input to $x$.

Adaptive connections may respond to carried flow
\cite{gross2008adaptive,berner2023adaptive,hu2013adaptation,stern2023learning},
but self-maintenance is a separate question \cite{montevil2015closure}.
We leave adaptation and persistence conditions unspecified.
