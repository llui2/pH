\section{Port-Hamiltonian dynamics}

Consider two states $x\in\mathbb R^n$ and $z\in\mathbb R^m$ with a
Hamiltonian $H(x,z)$. We keep the two states explicit and write
\begin{align}
\dot x
&=
[\mathbf J(x)-\mathbf R(x)]\nabla_x H
+\mathbf C(x,z)\nabla_z H
+\mathbf G(x)u,
\label{eq:x}\\
\dot z
&=
[\mathbf J(z)-\mathbf R(z)]\nabla_z H
-\mathbf C^\top(x,z)\nabla_x H,
\label{eq:z}
\end{align}
with
\begin{equation}
\mathbf J^\top=-\mathbf J,
\qquad
\mathbf R^\top=\mathbf R\succeq0.
\end{equation}
The matrices $\mathbf J$ generate conservative motion, $\mathbf R$
dissipates energy, and $\mathbf C$ transfers energy between $x$ and $z$.
The remaining port acts on $x$ through $\mathbf G(x)$, with output
\begin{equation}
y
=
\mathbf G^\top(x)\nabla_x H.
\label{eq:y}
\end{equation}

The opposite signs of the $\mathbf C$ terms make the $x$--$z$ coupling
power preserving. Along the full dynamics,
\begin{equation}
\dot H
=
-\nabla_x H^\top\mathbf R(x)\nabla_x H
-\nabla_z H^\top\mathbf R(z)\nabla_z H
+y^\top u.
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
Thus $\mathbf C$ only moves energy between the two states, while
$y^\top u$ is the power exchanged through the remaining port.

\section{Constrained feedback}

Let the desired configurations of $x$ form the manifold
\begin{equation}
\mathcal M
=
\{x:g(x)=0\},
\qquad
g:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^r.
\end{equation}
If $x$ starts on $\mathcal M$, invariance requires
\begin{equation}
\dot g
=
\partial_x g\,\dot x
=
0.
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:x},
\begin{equation}
\partial_x g\,\mathbf G\,u
=
-\partial_x g
\left(
[\mathbf J(x)-\mathbf R(x)]\nabla_x H
+\mathbf C(x,z)\nabla_z H
\right).
\label{eq:constraint}
\end{equation}

Define only the matrix that maps the port into the constraint coordinates,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf A(x)
=
\partial_x g(x)\mathbf G(x).
\label{eq:A}
\end{equation}
If $\mathbf A$ has full row rank, the port can correct every
constraint-violating direction. The general control is
\begin{align}
u
={}&
-\mathbf A^\dagger\partial_x g
\left(
[\mathbf J(x)-\mathbf R(x)]\nabla_x H
+\mathbf C(x,z)\nabla_z H
\right)
\nonumber\\
&+
\left(
\mathbf I-\mathbf A^\dagger\mathbf A
\right)v.
\label{eq:u}
\end{align}
The first term is the minimum-norm correction required to keep
$\dot g=0$. The second lies in $\ker\mathbf A$ and therefore does not
affect the constraint.

The same construction can make $\mathcal M$ attractive. Imposing
\begin{equation}
\dot g
=
-\mathbf K g,
\qquad
\mathbf K\succ0,
\end{equation}
gives
\begin{align}
u
={}&
-\mathbf A^\dagger
\left[
\partial_x g
\left(
[\mathbf J(x)-\mathbf R(x)]\nabla_x H
+\mathbf C(x,z)\nabla_z H
\right)
+\mathbf K g
\right]
\nonumber\\
&+
\left(
\mathbf I-\mathbf A^\dagger\mathbf A
\right)v.
\label{eq:restoring}
\end{align}

We now restrict the control to a self-feedback through the same port,
\begin{equation}
u
=
\mathbf Q(x,z)y,
\qquad
y
=
\mathbf G^\top(x)\nabla_x H.
\label{eq:self}
\end{equation}
The constraint becomes
\begin{equation}
\partial_x g
\left(
[\mathbf J(x)-\mathbf R(x)]\nabla_x H
+\mathbf C(x,z)\nabla_z H
+\mathbf G(x)\mathbf Q(x,z)y
\right)
=
0.
\label{eq:Qconstraint}
\end{equation}
Thus $\mathbf Q$ must map the available output $y$ to one of the control
vectors allowed by Eq.~\eqref{eq:u}. For $y\neq0$, one such map is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf Q
=
\frac{u y^\top}{y^\top y},
\label{eq:Q}
\end{equation}
since $\mathbf Qy=u$. If $y=0$, this algebraic self-feedback cannot
generate a nonzero correction.

The energetic effect of the feedback follows directly from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:energy},
\begin{equation}
y^\top u
=
y^\top\mathbf Qy.
\label{eq:Qpower}
\end{equation}
If $\mathbf Q^\top=-\mathbf Q$, the self-feedback is power preserving.
A negative symmetric part removes energy, while a positive symmetric
part injects it. Maintaining $g$ can therefore require either sign of
power. The matrix $\mathbf Q$ specifies how the available port is used;
it is not itself an energy reservoir. Any net energy supplied to or
removed from $x$ must ultimately be accounted for by the $z$ system or
by an external source or sink.
