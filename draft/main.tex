\title{Emergent organization in stochastic chemical networks}
\maketitle

\section{Physical reaction space}

Consider a well-mixed chemical system with molecule counts
$n=(x,z)\in\mathbb N_0^N$, where $x$ denotes its internal species and
$z$ the environmental resources and products. The admissible elementary
reactions are fixed by the chemistry. Reaction $r$ converts a reactant
complex $\alpha_r$ into a product complex $\beta_r$ and changes the
state by $\nu_r=\beta_r-\alpha_r$. The stoichiometric matrix collects
these changes. Equivalently, using the incidence matrix $\mathbf B$
of the graph of chemical complexes and their species-composition matrix
$\mathbf Z$, it is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf S=\begin{bmatrix}\nu_1&\cdots&\nu_M\end{bmatrix}
=\mathbf Z\mathbf B.
\label{eq:stoichiometry}
\end{equation}
The split $\mathbf S=[\mathbf S_x^\top,\mathbf S_z^\top]^\top$
distinguishes changes in the internal and environmental species
\cite{vanderschaft2013balanced}. Reservoir exchange, when present, acts
on $z$, not by directly replacing internal components.

Each reaction has a propensity $w_r(n)\geq0$. It vanishes when its
reactants are unavailable. For an elementary well-mixed reaction, the
stochastic mass-action propensity can be written as
\begin{equation}
w_r(n)=k_r\prod_{i=1}^{N}(n_i)_{\alpha_{ir}},
\qquad (m)_q=m(m-1)\cdots(m-q+1),
\label{eq:propensity}
\end{equation}
with $(m)_0=1$ and a rate constant $k_r$ fixed by the reaction
kinetics. A catalyst appears on both sides of a reaction and cancels
from $\nu_r$, but remains in $\alpha_r$ and hence in its propensity.
The current composition can therefore enable or suppress reaction
channels without changing the underlying chemical laws.

\section{Stochastic dynamics}

The process is a continuous-time Markov jump process. When reaction
$r$ fires, $n\mapsto n+\nu_r$; its conditional probability of firing
in a short interval $dt$ is $w_r(n)dt+o(dt)$. Its generator acts on an
observable $\varphi$ as
\begin{equation}
(\mathsf L\varphi)(n)
=\sum_{r=1}^{M}w_r(n)
\bigl[\varphi(n+\nu_r)-\varphi(n)\bigr].
\label{eq:generator}
\end{equation}
This follows the chemical master equation rather than an independently
chosen adaptation law \cite{gillespie1977,anderson2015}. The
\emph{enabled reaction set}
\begin{equation}
\mathsf A(n)=\{r:w_r(n)>0\}
\label{eq:enabled}
\end{equation}
changes when reactions produce or remove constituents needed by other
reactions. The generator is fixed as a function of $n$; what changes
along a trajectory is the effective set of active processes.

The propensities must also respect thermodynamics. For elementary
reversible channels, local detailed balance relates forward and reverse
rates to the free-energy change $\Delta H_r$ and chemical work $W_r$
provided by maintained reservoirs,
\begin{equation}
\log\frac{w_r(n)}{w_{\bar r}(n+\nu_r)}
=-\frac{\Delta H_r(n)-W_r}{k_{\mathrm B}T},
\label{eq:thermodynamics}
\end{equation}
where $\bar r$ denotes the reverse channel. The state free energy
includes mixing contributions; $W_r=0$ for an elementary reaction in
the explicitly modeled closed system. Catalysts can alter barriers and
rates without changing this thermodynamic balance
\cite{rao2016crn}.

\section{Objects of study}

The \emph{reaction-state graph} has configurations $n$ as vertices and
an edge $n\to n+\nu_r$ wherever $w_r(n)>0$. A state is reachable from
$n_0$ only if a finite sequence of such transitions exists. In
particular, a viable organization that cannot be assembled through
available reaction paths has zero probability of spontaneous formation.
This separates assembly from the ability to maintain an organization
once it has formed \cite{peng2022hierarchy}.

To examine persistence, let $C$ be a candidate set of essential
internal species and define
\begin{equation}
\Omega_C=\{n:n_i>0\ \text{for every }i\in C\},
\qquad
\tau_C=\inf\{t\geq0:n(t)\notin\Omega_C\}.
\label{eq:survival}
\end{equation}
For an initial state in $\Omega_C$, the survival probability
$\Pr_n(\tau_C>t)$ and mean exit time $\mathbb E_n[\tau_C]$ measure
how long the essential species remain present. The Markov generator
restricted to $\Omega_C$, with transitions leaving it treated as absorption, determines these
quantities. In particular, $T_C(n)=\mathbb E_n[\tau_C]$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
-\mathsf L T_C(n)=1\quad (n\in\Omega_C),
\qquad T_C(n)=0\quad (n\notin\Omega_C),
\label{eq:exit-time}
\end{equation}
whenever the mean is finite. For a finite killed state space, the
leading decay rate also controls long-time survival under standard
irreducibility assumptions. Presence alone is not maintenance:
we must additionally verify that internal reactions continually replace
degraded components using resources from $z$.

The problem is to determine which such organizations can be reached
from an initially unorganized state, and which become long-lived
without external replacement of their essential components. The
reaction constraints determine the possible dynamics; their products
in turn change which reactions are accessible. Chemical organization
theory and seed-dependent autocatalysis give complementary structural
starting points \cite{dittrich2007organization,montevil2015closure,peng2022hierarchy}.
